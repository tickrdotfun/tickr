# 20 · Reference versus production

The managed ticker system and the two activation buys were built from a reference that traded on the public chain on 2026-09-07 (PING priced in PONG, two activations, then a buy and a sell through an aggregator's router). This page lists every difference between that reference and what tickr deploys, why each exists, and what validates it. A difference is not a vulnerability, but nothing a difference touches inherits the reference's evidence; each is tested on its own. On the site the activation is called the listing: "listing pending" until the two buys land, "list your coin" for the creator.

## Not proven by the reference, said plainly

- The reference proves one sequence at one size on one day. It does not prove instant listing, listing at every size, dollar prices on every chart, or that any aggregator will route a given trade.
- A name's pool takes one visit per transaction. Split routes that cross it twice are unsupported and revert.
- The offer through a name's pool is finite: the floor plus four times the circulation, per swap. Larger buys wrap.
- A confirmed activation is "on-chain sequence confirmed, external trading unverified" until a real buy and sell through an outside router are recorded for that launch; the launch runbook records them.
- The site's first buy in a launch is paid in USDG the creator already holds (an approval, then the launch); the reference funded its setup differently. The order of the activation is the reference's; the count of signatures per mode is not promised.
- The fork test measures what the route put into the coin's pool from the route's own swap log, not from the wrapper's books, and then checks the wrapper's backing against that.

## After the R16 review

- **Unknown submissions** (R16-M01). An attempt whose hash the wallet never returned stays unresolved until the person pastes the hash the wallet shows, which is then matched against the reviewed request. Nothing about the chain's view of the wallet's nonce turns unknown into "never sent". The one definite decline is the wallet's own pre-broadcast rejection. A browser without a lock manager signs nothing. The workflow lives in `web/src/lib/activationFlow.ts` with every dependency injected, and `web/scripts/tests/activationFlow.test.cjs` drives it through a lost wallet answer, a transaction that appears late, a reload, a second tab, no lock manager, a decline, a pending receipt, a reverted purchase, an expired review, a moved nonce, a smart account, a zero or failing quote and a storage that does not keep writes.
- **Creation recovery** (R16-M02). A launch has a journal of its own (`web/src/lib/launchJournal.ts`): the seed, the predicted address and what the launch is are written before the wallet opens, the hash the moment the wallet returns it, the coin once its receipt is read. The receipt the runner waited for is reused, never fetched twice; a read that fails after the broadcast leaves the record with its hash and the page settles it, reads only, on the next visit; a wallet answer that was lost blocks new launches until the hash is matched or the person confirms the wallet shows nothing; a resumed launch keeps its seed, so a second signature could only be the same address, which cannot deploy twice. The transaction link stays visible on an error.
- **Completion for every mode** (R16-M03). Only a launch under an invented name enters the activation step; every other launch shows its receipt, the coin's address and the link to it.
- **Genesis** (R16-M04). Without the router and the quoter in the record, genesis refuses before it broadcasts anything. The only exemption is `GENESIS_ALLOW_NO_ACTIVATION`, refused on chain 4663, and it writes `genesisActivated: false` into the record.
- **Addresses** (R16-L01). The record is written in checksum form by the sync step and read in checksum form by the site; the example record is checksummed; the activation suites pass on the shipped tree as it is.
- **Labels and signals** (R16-L02). A stage is called confirmed only by a verified receipt; without an inspection it is unknown. The public signal treats an empty-code answer of `"0x"` or `undefined` as an ordinary wallet and a thrown read as unknown, finds the launch transaction from the pool's `Initialize` log rather than assuming the first swap, and the grid's mark is that same signal, asked only for fresh coins under a name.
- **Docs** (R16-L03). The wrapper's description in 05 is the managed one throughout: inventory against circulation, backing at or above circulation, conversion one for one while the pool manager is locked, and no claim of a guaranteed dollar or of routing elsewhere.
- **Calldata parity**. `UniversalRouterBuy.sol` encodes the same `ExactInputParams` struct the site encodes; `contracts/test/UniversalRouterBuy.t.sol` holds golden bytes produced by the site's encoder and fails on any byte that moves.

## After the R17 review

- **Creation recovery** (R17-M01). Creation now runs on its own engine (`web/src/lib/launchFlow.ts`) with the same discipline as the activation: the wallet's signing lock is held across tabs for the whole launch (shared with the activation, so a wallet signs one thing at a time on the site); the record is read and validated under that lock before anything is prepared; the intent carries the exact target, calldata, value, nonce, chain, wallet and predicted coin; the nonce is fixed right before the wallet opens; the hash is written the moment the wallet returns it, by the launch step alone, so an approval's hash can never be taken for the launch; an unresolved launch has no dismissal, it is settled by its hash, matched against the reviewed transaction (sender, target, calldata, value, nonce, chain), or by the wallet's own pre-broadcast rejection; a receipt settles a launch only when the factory itself emitted the launch of the predicted coin; a record this browser cannot read is unresolved, not absent. The transaction runner resets its per-run refs before any guard. Seven offline lifecycle cases in `web/scripts/tests/launchFlow.test.cjs`: intent before the wallet, a lost answer, old or mistaken hashes, another coin in the receipt, a reload, a second tab, no lock manager, damaged and unreadable storage, a write that does not land, an approval followed by a guard failure, a reverted launch.
- **The live schema** (R17-M02). The record's schema lives in one module (`web/scripts/lib/recordSchema.mjs`), used by the release guard and tested with a record shaped exactly as genesis writes it. `genesisActivated` is a field of the schema and a live build requires it to be `true`; a rehearsal record or an unactivated genesis is refused with its reason.
- **Displays** (R17-L01). Everything the activation hook reads or reviews is stamped with the wallet, chain and coin it was read for and is never shown for another identity, so a read that fails after a wallet change cannot leave a stale done. The public signal returns unknown when the scan is truncated (more swaps than it inspects and none of the inspected qualify), and the coin branch accepts a delivery only into an ordinary wallet, like the name branch.
- **Tests** (R17-L02). Every offline suite starts from one fixture (`web/scripts/tests/fixture.cjs`) and passes on its own against the checked-in zero record. The golden calldata lives in `golden.json`, is checked by the activation suite against the site's encoder and against the hex in the Solidity test, and `node scripts/tests/golden.cjs --write` regenerates both after a reviewed change.

## After the R18 review

- **Delayed settlement** (R18-M01). Every transition of the creation engine, the reads-only ones included (`settle`, `recover`, `clear`), runs under the wallet's signing lock across tabs, and every write re-reads the record and refuses when it is no longer the one the step started from, so a delayed result for an older launch can never overwrite a newer one. The stored record is validated in full by status: a sent or launched record without its hash, a launched record without its coin and block, an unknown status or a prepared record carrying a hash are refused as unresolved. The creation receipt is revalidated against its canonical block hash and waits for one more block, as the activation does.
- **Repricing** (R18-L01). The runner reports a repricing with the replacement's identity; the engine adopts it only when sender, target, calldata, value, chain and nonce are the reviewed ones, keeping the first hash alongside; a repricing into another request stops the runner for review; a repriced launch can also be matched by hand. `web/src/lib/txSteps.ts` holds the runner's settlement so the test drives the runner and the engine together.
- **Golden fixture** (R18-L02). The offline suites replace the checked-in deployment record with the example's zeros inside their own process, so the fixture's addresses are what every module sees on any machine; the golden bytes and the Solidity test's constants are regenerated from that fixture and checked by the activation suite.
- **Approvals** are signed inside the same lock as the launch; the documentation's claim that a wallet signs one thing at a time on the site now holds for the whole launch. An older record from a previous build (`tickr.launch.v1`) blocks a new launch until it is settled by hand. The runbook's checklist carries the acceptance gate the review asked for: a real buy and sell of the official coin through GMGN's router after its activations, plus a throwaway name and a second coin under it through the site, before the public switch.

## Upload protection, 2026-09-07

The image upload went through three states in one day. It began as a strict gate (twelve an hour and three a minute per address, and a build that could not reach its counter refused everything), which a hand test on the preview hit with nothing uploaded. It was then opened completely. An outside reviewer pointed out the same day that an origin header is not authentication and that an open route lets a script spend the pinning plan.

What stands now, in order, all of it invisible to a creator:

1. The origin check, advisory as before.
2. A signature from the creator's wallet: one free `personal_sign` per wallet per hour, kept in the tab, naming the wallet, the chain and the expiry. The server verifies it and asks the chain that the wallet holds at least 0.0001 ETH, so a script has to fund every address it uploads from. The preview, which replays a recording and has no wallet, skips this step and keeps everything else.
3. Shared counters in the tokens Worker, one Durable Object per name: thirty uploads an hour per address, thirty per wallet, two thousand an hour for everyone, and ten thousand a day as the hard spending budget. A site that cannot reach them counts per instance instead.
4. A memory of pinned files by content hash, so the same bytes are never sent to Pinata or counted twice.
5. One JSON log line per decision in the Worker's observability, and `GET /api/pin` shows the live counters.

Whenever the route says no, for any reason other than the file itself, the create page stores the image with the coin on-chain, the same path it takes where pinning is not set up, so a creator is never stopped and never sees a refusal. The old burst limiter was also found never to count in production (twenty calls in a row against a limit of ten all passed) and is gone.

## After the R19 verification, 2026-09-07

The verification of build 19 confirmed the three R18 repairs and left two creation-flow items, two disclosure items and the qualifications from the external review of the same day. Every item, and what was done:

- **R19-M01, creation could sign from a different wallet than the one reviewed.** Fixed. The runner takes the reviewed identity (`RunOptions` in `web/src/hooks/useTx.ts`): right before each wallet request it asks the connector for its live account and network and stops, with nothing signed, when either differs; and it binds `account` and `chainId` into the request itself. Creation passes the reviewed wallet and chain for both the approval and the launch.
- **R19-L01, a failure before the launch was asked of the wallet stranded the journal.** Fixed. The decision after a run is one pure function, `afterRun` in `web/src/lib/launchOutcome.ts`, with its own offline suite: a run that never invoked the wallet's send is "not sent" and clears the prepared record; a post-approval simulation failure does the same; only a run that reached the wallet keeps an unknown outcome. The runner's own pre-send checks (the factory-code check, the identity check) fall on the "not sent" side.
- **R19-L02, fee and buyback text.** Fixed: the struct comments in `06-fees.md`, and the two places in `SPEC.md` that still described the buyback split as 80/20. The shares are 50/10/40 under a name, 60/0/40 elsewhere; the treasury starts half and half and the burn share can only rise.
- **R19-L03, preview addresses.** Fixed: the preview build says whose addresses they are and shows them without explorer links.
- **The optional first buy's minimum.** Tightened: a first buy whose preview rounds to nothing is refused before anything is prepared, and the request is never built with a zero minimum. The preview is a pure function of the amount and the economics pinned into the transaction, so there is no clock to expire; a change in economics reverts the launch instead.
- **Lint, headers, dependencies, CI.** `eslint .` is clean (the build output is ignored, the CommonJS suites are allowed to `require`). Security headers ship from `next.config.ts`. Overrides live in `pnpm-workspace.yaml`, where pnpm 10 reads them, with `uuid` pinned; `pnpm audit` is clean. A workflow runs the contracts (build with sizes, unit, fuzz at a thousand runs, invariants), the site (audit, lint, suites, golden bytes, build, typecheck) and the tokens Worker on every push.
- **Owner, epoch, anti-snipe, capacity, Mode 5, legacy records.** Accepted as designed and disclosed: the owner's three-day fee-recipient reassignment stays (the takeover path) and the runbook now puts the owner behind a Safe multisig; pots land in the window of the sweep (`05-anchors.md`); launch protection is a best-effort deterrent for standard routers (`02-lifecycle.md`, the token page); one wrapper-pool visit per transaction; Mode 5 stays off; legacy records are quarantined, not migrated.
- **The acceptance gate** stays a launch-day step in `LAUNCH.md`: real buys and sells through GMGN on the exact release, with hashes, before the public switch.
